# SLM_beam_propagation
MATLAB implementation for phase-mask generation to control spatial light modulators

## Overview
This study provides a documented implementation of a numerical workflow that converts a desired phase pattern into a complex optical field and to control the incident beam.

SLM is a pixelated device that modulates light phase, amplitude, or polarization and displays grayscale image. Each pixel controls a liquid crystal molecule which rotates and delays the light passing through it and the image displayed is called computer generated hologram (CGH). Beam shaping is controlling the spatial profile and intensity distribution of laser beam. The pulse duration is maintatined at femto seconds to picoseconds to make sure that pulse ends before heat transfers to the sorrounding leading to minimal thermal damage. The application includes micromachining, dental implants, aerospace structures, micro-optical elemnt fabricatioon.

# Beams considered for study
## 1. Gaussian Beam
A gaussian beam has a flat phase front naturally. The SLM applies a lens phase to focus it at focal length.
The ring spacing or span purely depends on wavelength and focal lenght. Shorter the wavelength, tighter the rings

Numerical equation : φ = −π / (λ·f) × r²

## 2. Flat-Top beam
A flat-top, or top-hat, beam has approximately uniform intensity over a defined target area. This differs from a Gaussian beam, which has maximum intensity at its centre and decreases gradually toward the edges.In this project, a spatial light modulator (SLM) applies a programmable phase mask to an incident Gaussian beam. The phase-modified complex optical field is propagated numerically in MATLAB. The purpose is to redistribute optical energy and generate a flatter output intensity profile.

Flat-top beams are useful in laser-material processing because a more uniform intensity distribution can improve consistency of energy delivery over the irradiated area.

## 3. Vortex Beam
A vortex beam is a structured optical beam with a helical phase distribution around its propagation axis. Unlike a conventional Gaussian beam, which has maximum intensity at the centre, an ideal vortex beam has a phase singularity at its centre.At the phase singularity, the optical phase is undefined. The field amplitude therefore approaches zero at the beam centre, producing a dark central region surrounded by a ring-shaped intensity distribution. This ring-shaped distribution is commonly described as a doughnut-shaped beam profile.

Numerical equation : φ = l × θ  (azimuthal phase ramp)

l = topological charge (integer).
Phase sweeps 0 to l×2π around the beam axis.
